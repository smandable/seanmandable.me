[Debt Descent](/debt-descent/) is a native debt-payoff planner for iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Add your debts, compare the Snowball and Avalanche methods, and see exactly when you'll be debt-free. There's no account or sign-up, and nothing is ever sent to me.

### Frequently asked questions

**Is my financial data private?**
Yes. Your data lives on your device and in your own private iCloud, where I can't see it. There are no accounts, analytics, or servers of my own. See the [Privacy Policy](/debt-descent/privacy/).

**Does my data sync between my iPhone, iPad, and Mac?**
Yes, automatically, and it's free for everyone. Just be signed in to iCloud with the same Apple Account on each device; there's nothing to set up in the app. If you're not signed in, Debt Descent works fully on-device, and syncing picks up when you sign in.

**I signed out of iCloud and the app looks empty. Is my data gone?**
No. When you sign out of iCloud (or turn iCloud off for Debt Descent), the system removes the synced copy from that device, which is standard for synced apps. Your data is still in your own private iCloud and comes right back when you sign in again. If you want a copy that stays on the device regardless, use **Export to JSON** before signing out.

**What does Debt Descent Pro include, and how much is it?**
Pro is a **one-time $14.99 purchase** (no subscription) that unlocks the five payoff methods beyond the free Snowball and Avalanche (one of them a custom order you set yourself), the what-if slider and per-debt payoff tests, **Pick a debt-free date** (see the extra it takes to finish by then), planned windfalls, refinance modeling (including applying the deal in one step), amortization schedules, PDF export of your plan and your payment history, calendar export, due-date and payday notifications, and the statement-day balance check. One purchase unlocks Pro on your iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Skipping a payment, saving up for something, notes on a debt, the year card, logging from the medium widget, paying a debt weekly or every two weeks, paydays and a bank balance, bills with due dates, autopay, clearing old payments, importing a plan, and exporting your history as a CSV are all free.

**I bought Pro but don't see it on a new device.**
It should unlock automatically when you're signed in with the same Apple Account. Pro is Family Sharing enabled too, so the members of your Family Sharing group get it on their devices without buying it again. If it doesn't appear, open the paywall (tap any "PRO" feature, or the ••• menu, then "Unlock Debt Descent Pro") and tap **Restore Purchases**.

**I have an offer code. Where do I enter it?**
In Settings, tap **Redeem a code…**, or tap **Redeem a Code** on the paywall. Apple's redeem sheet opens, and Pro unlocks once the code goes through.

**How do I import a statement?**
Tap **Import statement** under your debts on the Plan tab (or use File ▸ Import Statement… on the Mac) and pick a PDF, or a photo of the statement, which is how a Mac uses a Continuity Camera capture. On iPhone and iPad you can also tap **Scan** (beside "Add a debt") to photograph a paper statement with the camera. You can open a statement PDF straight from Files or Finder too. Either way, Debt Descent reads the balance, APR, minimum, and due date on your device, and shows you every field to confirm before anything is saved. It also takes the statement's closing day as the debt's statement day, sets up a card's intro rate (like 0% until a date) as a promo, matches the minimum the statement printed, and names a new card the way the statement does.

**Can it read my paystub?**
Yes. Open **Income & bills** and tap **Scan a paystub** to photograph one with the camera (iPhone and iPad), or **Read from a paystub…** to use a photo or a PDF. A review sheet shows the net pay and pay frequency it found so you can check both against the stub, and nothing is saved until you confirm. They land in Income & bills as your take-home pay and cadence. Like statement reading, it runs on your device, and the paystub is never uploaded.

**Can Debt Descent connect to my bank?**
No. There's no account and no bank linking, and nothing leaves your device. Balances come from your statements instead: import the PDF, scan the paper one, or update the balance by hand when the statement posts.

**One monthly payment covers several of my debts. Can Debt Descent track that?**
Yes, and payment groups are free for everyone. On the Payments tab, add a payment group, pick the debts it covers, and enter the monthly amount that reaches them. Log the payment once and Debt Descent splits it across the group to the cent, following the servicer's own schedule when every member has a fixed payment. If your plan keeps a fee (it drafts more than it sends to your creditors), record that in the **Plan fee** field. The app then matches your bank statement while keeping the fee out of your payoff math.

**A debt in a payment group shows a different due date than its statement. Why?**
Because that's the date your money goes out. A debt enrolled in a group shows the group's payment date everywhere: upcoming payments, reminders, calendar export. The creditor's own due date isn't overwritten; it comes back the moment the debt leaves the group.

**My student loan is in deferment. How do I enter it?**
Add or edit the loan and turn on **In deferment — nothing due yet**, then set the date the deferment ends. Until that date the loan sits out of your plan: nothing is due, it stays off upcoming payments and reminders, and the payoff math skips it. When the date arrives it rejoins the plan on its own. Under the date, **When payments start** decides what happens to your monthly total then. **Added to my monthly total** (the default) adds the loan's payment to it. **Comes out of my monthly total** takes the payment out of your extra instead (and out of payments rolling forward, if the extra isn't enough), so your total stays the same. If the plan can't cover all of it, your total rises by only the rest, and the Debt payments card says by how much. While every debt you still owe is deferred, the Debt payments card reads $0.00 a month and says when your extra starts. In a payment group, a deferred loan takes no share of the group's payment until its deferment ends. Enter the eventual monthly payment if you know it (the row shows it as "$150/mo after") or add it later. If the loan is subsidized (no interest accrues while it's deferred), also turn on **No interest during deferment (subsidized)** and the balance holds still until the date. Deferment is available for loans, not cards.

**I want to watch a balance without paying it off in the plan (a mortgage, a 0% loan).**
Choose **Track only (leave out of plan)** in the debt's menu (the ••• button on its row; right-click on the Mac, long-press on iPhone). The debt stays in your list with its balance and due date, and its row reads "Tracked · not in the plan", but the payoff math, your extra, and the debt-free date leave it out. It still counts in your totals and in the Where it's owed rings on the Progress tab, whose caption says how many debts are held out, like "(1 not in the plan)". Choose **Put back in plan** when you want it back in. Mortgage is one of the debt kinds, alongside cards, loans, and medical debt.

**Can I skip a month's payment?**
Yes, and it's free. On the Plan tab, open the debt's menu (the ••• button on its row; right-click on the Mac, long-press on iPhone and iPad) and choose **Skip this month's payment** or **Defer to next month**. You can also do it from the Payments tab: long-press a payment under Upcoming payments (right-click on the Mac) for **Log a payment**, **Skip this month's payment**, or **Defer to next month**. Before anything changes, Debt Descent tells you what it costs, like "Skipping costs about $38 in interest and moves debt-free to March 2029." A skip sends nothing to that debt this month. It keeps charging interest, and the plan picks back up next month. A defer does the same, then adds this month's minimum to next month's payment, on top of your budget. The debt's row then reads "Skipped · resumes in October" or "Deferred · $290.00 due in October", and Upcoming payments, Left over, and Debt payments show it too. History keeps a muted row on that month's due day that reads "Skipped" or "Deferred to October", and no total counts it. If you change your mind, the same menu has **Undo skip** or **Undo defer**. The app doesn't tell your lender anything, and it doesn't know their late fees, so please call your lender or card issuer first, since many will waive one late fee if you ask. Skip and Defer aren't offered for a debt in a payment group, a loan in deferment, a paid-off or tracked-only debt, a debt you've already logged a payment to this month, or the month a deferred payment comes due.

**What's the difference between "Defer to next month" and a loan in deferment?**
**Defer to next month** is your own call, for one month. This month's minimum moves to next month, and the debt keeps charging interest in between. A loan in deferment is a pause your lender has agreed to, often for months. You set that up by editing the loan and turning on **In deferment — nothing due yet**, with the date it ends. If your lender has granted a pause, use the loan deferment. If you're only moving one payment, use Defer.

**Why does a debt show up more than once in Upcoming payments?**
Starting with 1.18, Upcoming payments lists every due date in the stretch it covers. If a debt comes due again before the last payment in the list, that later date shows up too, so the payments are in the order they happen. The repeat rows are muted, show the regular amount, and have no log button, so log the payment from the debt's first row. The card shows the next six due dates, and a line like "2 more after Oct 28" covers the rest. When the card is folded, its count includes every one.

**Can I see what each paycheck has to cover?**
Yes. On the Plan tab, tap **Edit** on the Left over card to open Income & bills, and set your next **Payday**. Upcoming payments then lists each payment under the paycheck that covers it, with headers like "Payday · Fri, Oct 9 · $412 to pay". A "~" in front of a figure means it's an estimate.

**Can it tell me if what's in the bank covers my payments?**
Yes, if you type the balance in. Under **Payday** in Income & bills, the **Bank** row takes what's in your account today. Upcoming payments then says whether it covers what's due before your next payday, like "$900.00 in the bank · $488.00 to spare" or "$300.00 in the bank · $112.00 short". Debt Descent can't see your account, so the figure is an estimate that knows only the number you entered, the payments you log, and the bills you mark paid. After your next payday it waits for a new balance. The Bank row is optional and free, and it syncs to your other devices but isn't in the JSON backup.

**Can I add due dates to my bills?**
Yes. In Income & bills, tap **Add a due date** under a bill, and it then reads like "Due Nov 1". With a due date, a bill is listed in Upcoming payments too, where a check marks it paid. It comes around again at the bill's own frequency. Marking a bill paid doesn't log anything to your plan, since bills aren't debts, and one whose date passes without a check reads "Past due". Each paycheck's total and the bank line include your bills.

**Can Debt Descent log the payments I have on autopay?**
Yes, and it's free. Turn on **On autopay** for a debt by choosing **Edit details** in the debt's menu (the ••• button on its row), or turn it on for a payment group or a bill. On each due date, Debt Descent logs the payment the next time it opens, dated that day, and marks a bill paid. It logs the debt's own payment (a card's minimum), so any extra your plan sends it is still yours to log. It doesn't move any money. If you've already logged part of a debt's payment, it logs only what's left, and nothing once it's all paid. A payment group's autopay logs nothing once the group's payment is logged, and a payment made to one debt in the group doesn't count toward it. A payment you delete in History stays deleted, and two devices won't both log the same one. Upcoming payments and the debt's row on the Plan tab say "On autopay".

**Can I save up for something without putting it on a card?**
Yes, and it's free. Once your income or bills are in, the Left over card on the Plan tab has **Save up for something**. Say what it's for, what it costs, how much you'll set aside and how often (a week, every 2 weeks, twice a month, or a month), and when you start. The card then shows the day it's yours, like "Yours by November 19", with a bar like "$500.00 of $1,200.00 · $100.00 a week". Beside it, Debt Descent shows what charging it to your highest-rate card would cost in interest over the rest of your plan, and how much sooner the same money would make you debt-free if it went toward debt instead. While you're saving, Left over has a row like "Saving for New laptop", so what's left accounts for it. When it's ready, tap **I bought it** and it comes off the card.

**Can I keep notes on a debt?**
Yes. Open the debt's menu (the ••• button on its row; right-click on the Mac, long-press on iPhone and iPad), choose **Edit details**, and use **Notes** for things like a promo's fine print or who you spoke to and when. Notes sync to your other devices and go in your backups, and nothing in the plan reads them.

**Can I pay a debt weekly or every two weeks?**
Yes, and it's free. Choose **Edit details** in the debt's menu (the ••• button on its row) and set **Paid** to Twice a month, Every 2 weeks, or Weekly, with the **Days** it's paid (twice a month) or the date the first payment is **Starting**. Each payment is half the monthly payment, or a quarter of it weekly, so a $300 car payment becomes $150 every two weeks. Paid every two weeks, that's 26 half payments a year, the same as 13 monthly payments instead of 12, which is how the debt clears sooner. Each date gets its own row in Upcoming payments, and a due-date reminder comes on each payment date. A debt in a payment group stays monthly, on the group's schedule.

**My card's minimum is 1% of the balance plus interest. Can Debt Descent match it?**
Yes. Choose **Edit details** in the debt's menu (the ••• button on its row) and set **Rule** to **Percent plus interest**. Most large US card issuers figure the minimum as 1% of the balance plus the month's interest and fees. The other rules are **Percent of balance** and **Fixed amount**. New cards start on Percent plus interest with a $25 floor, and cards already in the app keep the rule they had.

**Can I keep my extra away from one debt?**
Yes. Choose **Edit details** in the debt's menu (the ••• button on its row) and turn on **Takes only its payment — no extra**. The plan then pays that debt its own payment and nothing more, whatever method you use, and your extra goes to the others. Tap **Add an end date** if it's only for a while, and the debt's row reads like "Takes only its payment until Jan 2027". To pay it a set amount, put that amount in the debt's **Payment** field and turn this on.

**Can I see how much of a loan I've paid off?**
Yes. Choose **Edit details** in the debt's menu (the ••• button on its row) and fill in **Borrowed** and **Started**, what the loan started at and when. Both are optional. The loan's row then shows how much of it is paid off and how far ahead of its original term your plan finishes, like "40% of the original $24,000 paid off" and "1 yr 5 mo ahead of its original term".

**I logged a payment, but the balance doesn't include the interest my card charged.**
Debt Descent treats your balance as "what your statement says": logging a payment takes exactly what you paid off the tracked balance, and the app never adds its own interest estimate, because card interest depends on average daily balance, posting dates, and fees, so any figure the app invented would drift from your actual statement. When your next statement posts, update the balance (edit the debt, or import the statement) and that catches the interest charge. Your *plan* (the debt-free date, total interest, and method comparisons) does fully model interest at each debt's APR; only the live balance stays anchored to your statements, so you can always verify it against them.

**How does History decide what counts as extra?**
It works it out from the amounts. Each month, a debt's payments count toward that month's minimum first, and whatever goes beyond it is extra, so there's no box to tick when you log a payment. A payment made once the month's minimum was already covered gets an **EXTRA** badge, and History's footer splits what you've paid into "Toward the minimums" and "Extra, beyond the minimums". The CSV's extra column holds the same split, payment by payment.

**What is the statement-day balance check?**
It's a reminder when each new statement is in, so you enter the real balance and the plan runs on it. If a debt has a statement day (open its ••• menu, choose **Edit details**, and set **Statement**, under Due day), the reminder comes two days after it. Otherwise it comes a few days after the due day. Importing a statement fills in the statement day from its closing date. The **Final-statement check after a payoff** comes two days after the statement day too, or 30 days after the payoff if the debt has none. Both are part of Pro. In Settings ▸ Notifications, turn on **Statement-day balance check** (it's off by default).

**Can I get a notification on payday?**
Yes, with Pro. In Settings ▸ Notifications, turn on **Payday notification** (it's off by default). At 9 AM on each payday it tells you what that paycheck has to cover, the payments and bills due before the next one, like "Payday: $412 to pay from this paycheck". It needs your next payday in Income & bills.

**Can I log a payment from a reminder?**
Yes. Starting with 1.20.2, a due-date reminder has a **Log payment** button (**Log payments** when it names several debts) that logs this month's payment without opening the app. On the debt that gets your extra, the reminder gives both figures, like "About $156 due in a few days. Your plan pays $599." A payment group's reminder has no button, so log the group's payment in the app. Due-date reminders are part of Pro.

**Why didn't I get a milestone card for a debt I paid off?**
Milestone cards on the Plan tab mark your first payment, a quarter, half, and three quarters paid off, a debt paid off, and debt-free, and they only cover the last 45 days. A payoff from before you installed 1.16 won't get one. It's still in your Payments history, and the next milestone you reach gets its card. Each card has a **Share your progress** button, and you can dismiss it once you've seen it.

**What are the two rings on the Progress tab?**
"Where it's owed" shows where your balance sits: by kind (cards, loans, and so on) and by debt. Tap a slice, or hover over it on the Mac, to see which debt it is and its share.

**What's the dashed line on the Progress chart?**
Starting with 1.20, the chart has a line called "Your plan from today". It picks up where your Actual line ends and runs to your debt-free month, beside the gray Baseline plan, which is the plan you started with. The caption says when your plan from today reaches zero, and how far ahead of or behind the baseline that is.

**Can I share how my year went?**
Yes. On the Progress tab, tap the share button and pick **Your year** at the top of the sheet. The card shows what you've paid this year, the debts you've cleared, the interest you've saved, and your debt-free month, and **Include amounts** decides whether the dollar figures go on it. It's there once you've paid something or cleared a debt this year.

**Can I see what it takes to be debt-free by a certain date?**
Yes, with Pro. On the Plan tab, tap **Pick a debt-free date** at the foot of the **Extra toward debt each month** card. Pick a date beside **To finish by** and it tells you the extra you'd need each month, like "$215 extra a month gets you there with Avalanche." **Use** sets it for you. The date field shows the last day of the month you pick, because the plan clears by month, and until you pick one it follows your plan. It works from your plan as it stands, with skips, deferrals, and windfalls included. If the extra it needs is more than you have left over each month, it says that too, and there's no **Use** button. If your plan already gets there, it reads like "Your plan already clears by Jun 2027." or, with an extra set, "Your $215 extra a month gets you there: debt-free Jan 2029." If no amount can (a loan still in deferment can hold the date back), it gives you the earliest month your plan can clear. Tap **Pick a debt-free date** again to fold it away.

**Can I use Siri or Shortcuts?**
Yes. Ask Siri "When am I debt-free in Debt Descent" or "How much interest am I saving in Debt Descent", or say "Log a payment to [debt name] in Debt Descent" to record one. The Shortcuts app has those actions plus "Set my [debt] balance" and "Add a debt", so you can chain them into your own shortcuts. On the Mac the same actions appear in Spotlight. Your debts are in Spotlight as well: type a card's name to see its balance, and select it to open the debt in the app.

**Is there a widget?**
Yes, for the home screen, the lock screen, and the Mac desktop. It shows your debt-free date, what's left, and how far along you are. On the medium widget, the button logs your next payment without opening the app. It names the payment, like "Log $120.35 to Visa · Sep 28", then shows "Logged $120.35 to Visa" with **Undo**. Debt Descent records it right away if it's running (on the Mac, even in the background), or the next time you open it. When there's no payment to log yet, the button reads **Log payment** and opens the app. The widget reads a snapshot the app writes, so after you install 1.16, open Debt Descent once and the widget fills in. With no debts yet it says "Add a debt".

**Can the widget count down in days instead of months?**
Yes. Long-press the widget, choose Edit Widget, and set **Count in** to Days, and the number ticks down each morning.

**When a debt is paid off, does its payment move to my next debt?**
Yes, unless you turn that off. Rolling the payment forward is how Snowball and Avalanche finish sooner. If you'd rather keep that money each month, turn off **Roll payments forward** in Settings ▸ Plan. Each debt then pays its own minimum, a paid-off debt's payment leaves your plan, and only your extra (if you've set any) moves between debts. It's free, and the setting syncs to your other devices. With it on, you can still stop one paid-off debt's payment from rolling forward by choosing **Stop rolling minimum payment forward** in that debt's menu (the ••• button on its row; right-click on the Mac, long-press on iPhone and iPad).

**Can I watch today's interest add up?**
Yes. In Settings ▸ Plan, turn on **Interest per day tile** (it's off by default). The Plan tab then shows what your debts cost in interest each day, with how much has built up so far today beside it.

**What does the Plan summary card show?**
It shows the next 30 days of interest across your debts, and what the whole plan costs. In Settings ▸ Plan, turn on **Plan summary card** (it's off until you do).

**Is there a menu bar item on the Mac?**
Yes. In Settings ▸ Plan, turn on **Show in the menu bar**. Your method's glyph and your debt-free month sit in the menu bar, and clicking them shows what's left with a **Log Payment** button.

**How do I make the text bigger?**
On iPhone and iPad, Debt Descent follows your system text size (Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Display & Text Size ▸ Larger Text), and every screen and sheet reflows with it, up to the largest accessibility sizes. The widgets follow it too, up to the first accessibility size. On the Mac that setting doesn't reach apps like this one, so Debt Descent has its own **Text size** slider in Settings ▸ Appearance.

**Does it work with VoiceOver?**
Yes. Every field says what it's for, and each debt, payment, and balance reads as one sentence, with its buttons as separate stops. If something reads wrong, please let me know which screen.

**Why are amounts in £ or € instead of $?**
Amounts follow your device's region, so the app shows £, €, CA$, and so on where that's the local currency. The app's text is in English either way.

**How do I export my payment history?**
On the Payments tab, tap the **•••** on the History card. **Export to CSV…** is free, and the file opens in Excel or Numbers with each payment's date, debt, the debt's kind, amount, how much of it went beyond that month's minimum, whether it paid the debt off or closed it into a refinance, its note, and its payment group. The payoff and refinance columns say yes or no, so a spreadsheet can leave a refinance out of a total the way History's footer does. **Export to PDF…** is part of Pro. It lists one payment per line, newest first, with the History card's totals at the bottom. The Plan tab's ••• menu has the same two, **Export payment history (PDF)…** and **Export payment history (CSV)…**. When you make the PDF, you pick **Just the payments** or **Include my plan**. Include my plan puts your whole plan inside the PDF so it imports back into Debt Descent. Anyone you send that PDF to can open the plan too, so pick Just the payments for a copy you'll hand to someone else.

**Is my whole plan inside the PDFs I export?**
For the plan PDF, yes. Starting with 1.18, **Export plan (PDF)…** always puts your whole plan inside the PDF as an attached file, so Debt Descent can import it back, and the page says so. It includes your debts and payments and their notes, payment groups, windfalls, extra, income, bills, and Save up goals. Anyone you send a plan PDF to can open that attachment, in Debt Descent or in a PDF reader like Adobe Acrobat. The payment history PDF only carries your plan if you pick **Include my plan**.

**Can I clear out old payments?**
Yes. On the Payments tab, tap the **•••** on the History card and choose **Clear older than…**. Pick a date, or tap **Before this year** or **Older than a year**, and the sheet shows how many payments that is, their total, and the first few. Payments from this month always stay. Clearing only removes the rows from History. Your balances stay where they are, the Progress chart keeps its shape, and History's footer keeps one line like "Cleared earlier — 120 payments before Jan 1, 2026" with the total. It can't be undone, so if you want a copy, tap **Save these 12 payments as a CSV first** (the button shows your count) before you clear. In January the sheet suggests saving last year's payments as a CSV and then clearing them. A clear syncs to your other devices.

**Can I open a plan file from Files, Mail, or Finder?**
Yes. On iPhone and iPad, open the file from Files, Mail, or Messages, tap Share, and pick Debt Descent. On the Mac, use Finder's **Open With** ▸ Debt Descent, or drag the file onto the app's icon in the Dock. It works with a JSON backup and with a PDF that carries a plan, including a plan PDF from the app and the **Download for Debt Descent** and **Save as PDF** files from the [calculators on this site](/debt-descent/#free-tools). If you already have a plan, Debt Descent asks whether to **Add to my plan** or **Replace everything**. A PDF without a plan in it goes to the statement reader instead. Debt Descent shows up as an option for these files, but it never becomes your default app for them. On a fresh install, the Welcome screen has **Import a plan file** too.

**How do I back up my data?**
Use **Export to JSON** (••• menu / File menu) to save a complete backup, and **Import plan (JSON or PDF)…** (same menus) to restore it on any device. A plan PDF the app exported works too, since it carries the plan inside it. If the device already has a plan, the import asks whether to add to it or replace everything. Starting with 1.20, a backup also carries skipped and deferred months, History's record of cleared payments, your Save up goals, and each debt's note and statement day. Backup and restore are free for everyone. Your data is never behind the paywall. (iCloud sync keeps your devices in step, but it isn't a backup. Deleting a debt removes it everywhere, so an occasional JSON export is still the safety net.)

**How do I start over?**
Use **Purge All Data** (••• menu) to erase everything and start blank. On an empty app the menu also offers **Load Sample Data**, a made-up demo set. It only appears while there are no debts, so it can't replace your data by accident.
